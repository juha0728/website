Case study · Personal health & wellness web app

A wellness app that teaches, and never diagnoses.

Vitality gives people a calm place to check in on their day, build a health profile, and find educational ideas for food and exercise. This page follows the project from a written brief to the dashboard people see today.

Stage 1 · The question

How do you personalize health guidance without giving medical advice?

The brief set one hard limit before any screen existed. Everything on the site is educational. It does not diagnose, and it does not prescribe. Every later decision had to respect that line.

What the written brief asked for

Stage 2 · Ideation

The brief described the flow and the pages. A second set of prompts described how each area should work and feel. The overall style stayed the same for every area: modern, minimal, flat, sans-serif, professional and calming but energetic.

Stage 3 · User flow

One home screen that leads everywhere

People open the site, log in or continue as a guest, and land on the dashboard. From there they can check in, work on their health profile, browse Diet or Exercise, look at Statistics, or search. Finishing the profile makes the recommendations more relevant.

Stage 4 · The dashboard

What is on the home screen

  • Sidebar navigation with an icon for each page: Dashboard, My Health Profile, Daily Check-In, Diet, Exercise and Statistics. Settings and Log out sit below, next to the guest profile.
  • Guest mode note in the sidebar: browsing as a guest keeps data on this computer.
  • Greeting and search. A welcome message and a search box with example searches.
  • Educational notice at the top of the page, before any content.
  • Featured meal. A card with tags such as Vegan and Healthy Choice, the meal name, and quick facts for calories, preparation time and protein, plus a link to see all meals.
  • This week's check-ins. Seven day circles from Monday to Sunday with today marked, a status of done or not done yet, a short prompt about mood, energy, sleep and water, and a button to check in.
  • Health profile completion. A percentage and progress bar, a prompt to complete the profile for more relevant recommendations, and a button to start it.

The design uses rounded cards and pill buttons, large readable headings, an icon for every page, and plenty of white space, so the home screen stays easy to scan.

Stage 6 · Safety by design

Educational information only

The notice shown at the top of the dashboard

Educational information only. Vitality is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Always talk to a qualified healthcare professional about your health or any symptoms.

The brief set the rules: no diagnosis, no prescribing, and a clear pointer to a qualified healthcare professional. The notice sits above everything else so people read it before they read any suggestion.

Stage 7 · Reflection

What I took from building it

Why a health project

Biology, science, health, working out and self-care are the subjects I care about most. A wellness app let me bring all of them together in one project.

The hardest decision

Balancing personalization with user safety. I wanted recommendations that fit each person, while respecting their privacy and keeping the site in its role as an educational tool that never gives potentially unsafe medical advice.

What I would add

More detail in the diet section, with cooking tutorials and photos for each meal, and tutorials for the workout routines so they are easier to follow.
